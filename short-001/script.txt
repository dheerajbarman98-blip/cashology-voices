Your credit card's minimum payment isn't a goal. It's an anchor.

Say you owe three thousand dollars at twenty-four percent. A common formula, interest plus one percent, makes the minimum about ninety dollars. That feels manageable, so that's the number you pay.

Psychologists call this anchoring. A twenty eleven study found that just showing the minimum payment can pull what people repay down by as much as twenty-four percent.

Here's the cost. Pay only the minimum, charge nothing new, and it takes about fifteen years and almost five thousand dollars in interest. Pay about one hundred eighteen dollars a month instead, and you're done in three years, with about twelve hundred dollars in interest.

Your statement has a box showing how long the minimum takes, and what paying it off in three years costs. Look at it, then pick your own number.

The minimum is the least the bank will accept. It isn't the best price you can pay.
