Why does a credit card feel cheaper than cash?

Picture handing over two twenties for dinner. That stings. Now picture tapping a card. No sting at all.

Researchers say cards separate the fun of buying from the pain of paying. In a famous MIT experiment, students bid on tickets to a sold-out Celtics game. Half were told they'd pay in cash. Half were told they'd pay by credit card.

The cash group bid an average of twenty-nine dollars. The credit card group bid sixty-one.

Same tickets. Different payment method. The effect doesn't show up in every purchase, but it was biggest for things like tickets, where the price is hard to judge.

So try this. Before you tap, ask yourself: would I hand over cash for this? If the answer is no, wait a day.

A card makes spending easy. You get to decide when it's too easy.
